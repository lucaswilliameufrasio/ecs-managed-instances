## Capacity signals

- Best measured stage: **569,114.73 req/s** at 4,096 connections. At 8,192 connections throughput fell to `548,006.65 req/s` while p95/p99 latency rose to `85.535/114.182 ms`; this is a throughput plateau, not evidence that 598k is impossible.
- The simple estimate `8 × 74,828 req/s = 598,626 req/s` was within about `5%` of the observed peak. It was an extrapolation, not a measured ALB or ECS limit.
- AWS/ApplicationELB `CapacityUtilization` reached `100%` for seven consecutive one-minute datapoints (`21:13–21:19 UTC`) during the high-throughput stages. AWS describes 100% as possible when demand briefly exceeds automatic ALB scaling capacity; this is evidence the ALB may have constrained the run. The load balancer had no LCU reservation.
- There were no reported rejected connections, ALB 5XXs, or target connection errors. ALB `RequestCount` and target 2XX counts agree with the successful Oha requests.
- The load generator's EC2 CPU metric peaked at `68.4%` in the available five-minute samples, so CPU saturation of the `m9g.2xlarge` was not observed. This does not rule out a generator network, socket, or Oha limit. ECS service CPU reached `100%` maximum.
- The 4,096-connection stage transferred about `27.3 MB/s` per Oha's rate measurement. The peak observed ALB `ProcessedBytes` minute was about `12.6 GB/min` (`~210 MB/s`); CloudWatch datapoints are minute buckets and do not exactly align to Oha stage boundaries.

No LCU reservation or other capacity setting was changed for this run, so the result reflects the default internal ALB configuration. The next isolation step, if desired, is a separate run with enough ALB reserved capacity and the same 8-task/load-generator setup; it should be compared against this unreserved baseline.

> Results come from Oha's JSON output. Requests go through the ALB and are distributed across healthy targets; tasks that become healthy during a stage can receive traffic immediately. No separate warm-up phase is included.
